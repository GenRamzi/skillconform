## How the document works

It defines a manual decision pipeline:

1. choose one of three content pillars;
2. select one approved title family;
3. select one thumbnail family;
4. research nearby YouTube results and primary sources;
5. score the idea out of 100;
6. reject ideas below 78/100;
7. validate the hypothesis later using CTR and audience retention.

## Strengths

- clear positioning and first-person promise;
- content allocation totals 100%;
- title and thumbnail constraints are easy to apply;
- the 78/100 gate prevents weak concepts from entering production;
- it distinguishes facts, assumptions, and fictional simulation;
- it explicitly avoids promising guaranteed views.

## Gaps before calling it a benchmark

- no benchmark video URLs, channel list, sample size, or capture date;
- no evidence table supporting “what the references proved”;
- no precise definition of “nearby” search results;
- no US-market, language, geography, or personalization controls for YouTube search;
- no formula for measuring originality beyond manual judgment;
- no scoring anchors for what earns 0, 5, 10, or 15 points;
- no logging schema for CTR, retention, impressions, traffic source, or test window;
- no inter-rater process to reduce subjective scoring differences.
